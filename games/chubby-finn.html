How do you play Chubby Finn?

Chubby Finn is a grid-based puzzle. You steer Finn around the board with the on-screen arrow buttons or your keyboard's arrow keys. Your goal is to push every ball onto its glowing target spot. The catch is the same one that makes all Sokoban games tick: a ball can only be pushed, never pulled. One careless shove can wedge a ball into a corner where nothing can rescue it, so think before you push.

On mobile, the same arrow buttons appear on the board, so the game is fully playable on a phone or tablet. No install, no account, no sign-up.

How many levels does Chubby Finn have?

The game runs through a series of hand-designed grids that grow tighter and trickier as you progress. Early levels teach one idea each; later ones stack several traps in the same small space. What matters more than the count: every level is short. A failed board costs only a few seconds to restart, so you learn by replaying, not by grinding. The exact level count depends on the current version — check inside the game.

What makes Chubby Finn different from classic Sokoban?

Classic Sokoban is pure box-to-goal planning: warehouse crates, grey grids, zero charm. Chubby Finn keeps the same push-and-plan rules but wraps them in a casual, friendly package:

  • Cute, clean art and a chubby seal hero instead of an anonymous crate-pusher
  • Bite-sized boards tuned for a coffee break, not a long session
  • Touch-friendly controls, so it plays as well on a phone as on a desktop

If you like the logic of classic Sokoban but want something you can pick up and drop in two minutes, Chubby Finn sits right between brain teaser and casual game.

What should a beginner know before the first level?

Five rules will get you through most boards:

  • Spot deadlocks before you push. A ball pushed into a corner, or against a wall with no way around it, can never be rescued.
  • Work backward. Picture the final push first — where does the last ball need to stand, and where must Finn stand to push it?
  • Never block your own standing space. A ball is useless if you can't get behind it.
  • Don't rush to move the nearest ball. The obvious first push is often the one that ends the run.
  • Restart early, not late. As soon as a proven deadlock appears, hit restart. A 10-second reset beats 5 minutes of pushing hopelessly.
